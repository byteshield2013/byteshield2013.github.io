<p>Many companies deploy their servers in Hong Kong hoping to improve the experience for users in China. But geographic proximity does not guarantee that traffic takes the shortest or most stable path. When users in China connect to overseas services, traffic still crosses carrier backbones, international gateways, and peering points, and congestion, detours, or packet loss on any of those segments can degrade website and application performance.</p>
<p>When planning services for China, the data center location is only half the story. Choosing the right acceleration route matters just as much. RIM focuses on cost efficiency, cross-border performance, and protection flexibility, while CN2 is built around low latency and stable routing for businesses with stricter connection-quality requirements.</p>
<p>This article compares RIM and CN2 across three dimensions: route architecture, DDoS scrubbing behavior, and suitable use cases, to help you choose the cross-border acceleration plan that fits your needs.</p>
<h2>What is a RIM route?</h2>
<p>RIM is a routing solution designed specifically for China cross-border connectivity. It uses nodes close to China, in locations such as Japan, South Korea, and Singapore, to improve transmission quality when users in China access overseas websites, APIs, or applications.</p>
<p>Compared with ordinary, unoptimized international routes, RIM significantly reduces the likelihood of detours, congestion, and packet loss. By deploying on overseas nodes near China rather than in distant regions like Europe or the US, it shortens part of the cross-border path and delivers a more stable experience without using any nodes inside mainland China.</p>
<p>RIM's core value is balancing cost efficiency with cross-border performance, which makes it a good fit for companies that want to improve their China service quality while keeping overall investment reasonable, such as corporate websites, content platforms, and SaaS applications.</p>
<h2>What is a CN2 route?</h2>
<p>CN2 is the next-generation carrier network built by China Telecom, providing high-quality, low-latency, and stable cross-border transmission for China. Compared with ordinary routing, CN2 uses premium dedicated-line routing resources that noticeably reduce the impact of congestion, route detours, and packet loss.</p>
<p>CN2's advantage is not just lower latency, but consistently stable routing quality. Even during peak hours it keeps jitter and latency fluctuation low, so logins, payments, account lookups, API calls, and real-time interactions stay smooth. With its premium cross-border routing resources, CN2 suits businesses that treat connection quality, low latency, and user experience as core requirements.</p>
<h2>How do RIM and CN2 differ under DDoS attack?</h2>
<p>Both RIM and CN2 deliver good cross-border connectivity in normal conditions. Under a large-scale DDoS attack, however, they differ significantly in where traffic is scrubbed and how well connection quality holds up.</p>
<p><strong>RIM (overseas scrubbing):</strong> When a volumetric attack hits, traffic is diverted to high-defense scrubbing nodes in the United States. The US offers enormous international bandwidth and mitigation capacity at a clear cost advantage, but user traffic has to detour across the Pacific, so latency rises noticeably during an attack.</p>
<p><strong>CN2 (overseas or Hong Kong scrubbing):</strong> CN2 dedicated lines can typically be paired with either US or Hong Kong scrubbing. With local scrubbing in Hong Kong, clean traffic returns to your origin over the shortest possible path, maintaining very low latency and an excellent experience even while the attack is ongoing.</p>
<h2>RIM or CN2: which fits your business?</h2>
<p>There is no absolute winner between RIM and CN2; they are designed for different needs. If you value overall return on investment and want a balance of cost, cross-border performance, and stability, RIM is an excellent choice. If your business demands very low latency and top connection quality, a CN2 dedicated line delivers a more consistent and stable cross-border experience.</p>

<h2>Cross-border acceleration is more than picking a line</h2>
<p>China cross-border acceleration is not simply a binary choice between RIM and CN2. It requires weighing your target market, business model, site architecture, and security risk together, because every one of those factors directly shapes the end-user experience.</p>
<p>Each option has its place: RIM suits companies balancing cost efficiency, cross-border performance, and protection flexibility, while CN2 suits services where low latency, high stability, and business continuity are critical. The ideal plan builds a complete acceleration and protection architecture around your actual needs.</p>
